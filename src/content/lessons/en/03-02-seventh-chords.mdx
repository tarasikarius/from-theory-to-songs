## The idea

Stack another 3rd on top of a triad and you get a **7th chord**, the color layer of harmony.

| Chord | Formula | Sound and where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| maj7 (Cmaj7) | 1 3 5 7 | Soft, dreamy. Ballads, neo-soul, bedroom pop |
| 7, "dominant" (C7) | 1 3 5 ♭7 | Bluesy, tense. The whole blues, and the V7 that pulls home |
| m7 (Cm7) | 1 ♭3 5 ♭7 | Smooth minor. Funk, R&B, mellow rock |
| m7♭5, "half-diminished" | 1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭7 | Melancholy tension. The ii chord of minor keys |
| dim7 | 1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭♭7 | Maximum drama, symmetrical. A passing chord |
| m(maj7) | 1 ♭3 5 7 | Film noir, spy themes |

**Why the dominant 7th pulls so hard.** It contains a **tritone** between its 3rd and its ♭7. In G7 those notes are B and F. The tritone resolves by half steps in opposite directions into the tonic chord: B goes up to C and F goes down to E. That resolution **is** the V7–I cadence.

<progression-player tonic="C" numerals="ii7 V7 Imaj7 Imaj7" tempo="88"></progression-player>

**Extensions (9, 11, 13)** are more stacked 3rds, which is the same as scale degrees 2, 4 and 6 an octave higher. For rock and pop you mostly need:

- **add9** (1 3 5 9): a triad with the 9th added and no 7th. The shimmering pop chord.
- **m9** and **9**: a 7th chord plus the 9th.
- The knowledge that you can **leave out the 5th**, and on guitar often the root too. The 3rd and the 7th carry the identity of the chord.

**Sus chords** replace the 3rd with a neighbor: sus2 (1 2 5) and sus4 (1 4 5). With no 3rd they are neither major nor minor: open and ringing. Dsus4 – D – Dsus2 – D is half of the acoustic-rock songbook.

## On your instrument

**Open chords, upgraded.** Every open chord you know is one finger away from its 7th versions. Strings are listed from 6 to 1, and x means "do not play".

| Triad | maj7 | Dominant 7 | Minor | m7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C: x32010 | Cmaj7: x32000 | C7: x32310 | | |
| A: x02220 | Amaj7: x02120 | A7: x02020 | Am: x02210 | Am7: x02010 |
| D: xx0232 | Dmaj7: xx0222 | D7: xx0212 | Dm: xx0231 | Dm7: xx0211 |
| E: 022100 | Emaj7: 021100 | E7: 020100 | Em: 022000 | Em7: 020000 |
| G: 320003 | Gmaj7: 320002 | G7: 320001 | | |

**Shell voicings** use only the root, 3rd and 7th. They sound sophisticated, stay clean under gain, and leave room for a vocal or a lead.

```
Root on string 5        Root on string 6
     Cmaj7  C7   Cm7         Gmaj7  G7   Gm7
G|----4-----3----3----|  G|----4----4----3----|
D|----2-----2----1----|  D|----4----3----3----|
A|----3-----3----3----|  A|-------------------|
E|--------------------|  E|----3----3----3----|
```

Notice how little changes between the three types: one note moves by one fret each time.

**Piano.** Comp with the root in the left hand and the 3rd and 7th (the "guide tones") in the right. For ii–V–I in C: Dm7 is D with F and C. G7 is G with F and B. Cmaj7 is C with E and B. Only one right-hand note moves at each change.

## Worked example

The same loop, plain and upgraded:

| Version | Chords | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | Am – F – C – G | Solid, anonymous |
| Upgraded | Am7 – Fmaj7 – Cadd9 – Gsus4 → G | Softer, wider, more personal |

On guitar the upgraded version is easier, not harder: Am7 is x02010, Fmaj7 is xx3210, Cadd9 is x32030, Gsus4 is 320013 resolving to 320003. Several notes ring through all four chords, and that is exactly why it sounds connected.

## Exercises

1. Take Am – F – C – G and play the upgraded version from the worked example. Record both and describe the emotional difference in one sentence.
2. Find the tritone inside the open G7 shape: B on string 5 and F on string 1. Move each one a half step to its resolution in a C chord. Then find the tritone of E7 (G♯ and D) and resolve it into Am.
3. Write a four-chord progression where every chord is a sus2, sus4 or add9. This is the shimmering indie and post-rock sound: The Edge, plus delay.
4. Play a 12-bar blues in A with shell voicings only: A7, D7 and E7.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing maj7 and 7.** Cmaj7 has B. C7 has B♭. One is a dream and the other is a question.
- **Confusing add9 and 9.** Cadd9 has no 7th. C9 is a dominant chord with a ♭7 in it, and it has a completely different job.
- **Using 7th chords everywhere.** Under heavy distortion most of them turn to noise. Module 3.3 covers what works there.
