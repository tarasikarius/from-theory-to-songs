## The idea

Arrangement is how you draw the energy map from Module 7. There are five main controls.

**1. Addition and subtraction.** The cheapest drama is muting layers. Verse 2 is verse 1 plus one element. The last chorus is everything. The bridge strips down to two elements. And the oldest trick of all: drop everything out for one beat before the final chorus.

**2. Rhythmic density.** Keep the chords and change the subdivision. Guitar moving from 8ths to 16ths, or drums moving from hi-hat to crashing ride, lifts a section without a single new note.

**3. Register spread.** Intimacy is narrow and low. A climax is wide: low bass and high lead at once.

**4. Light and shade.** The heavy parts are only as heavy as the quiet parts allow. Led Zeppelin built a career on this. In metal: a clean, arpeggiated verse, then a wall-of-gain chorus.

**5. The repetition budget.** A listener accepts a section about three times unchanged. The fourth needs a new layer, a variation, or to be the last.

## On your instrument

**One guitar, five dynamic levels,** without touching the volume knob:

| Level | How to play |
|---|---|
| 1 | Single notes, palm-muted |
| 2 | Arpeggiated chords, let ring |
| 3 | Palm-muted power chords in 8ths |
| 4 | Open power chords or triads, 8ths |
| 5 | Full chords, 16ths, hitting hard |

Play one progression at each level. Then play it as a song: level 2 for the verse, 3 for the pre-chorus, 5 for the chorus, 1 for the bridge.

**Pick dynamics.** How hard you hit the strings changes tone as well as volume. With a slightly overdriven amp, a soft pick stroke is nearly clean and a hard one distorts. This is the most expressive control you have, and it costs nothing.

## Worked example

One 8-bar progression, Am – F – C – G twice, arranged three ways:

| Version | Guitar | Piano or second guitar | Drums | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ballad | Fingerpicked arpeggios, one chord per bar | Soft high chords on beat 1 | Brushes, or nothing | Intimate |
| Anthem | Open chords in 8ths, big strum | Octave melody above | Rock beat, crashes on each chord | Wide and triumphant |
| Tense | Muted 16ths on one note per chord | Sparse stabs on the off-beats | Kick and hi-hat only, no snare | Coiled, waiting to break |

Nothing about the harmony changed. The song's emotion is in the arrangement.

## Exercises

1. Arrange the same 8-bar progression three ways: (a) ballad, (b) anthem, (c) tense. Record 30-second sketches of each.
2. Take a favorite record and write down, chorus by chorus, what was **added** each time. Steal the pattern for the final chorus of Assignment Song 1.
3. Play Assignment Song 1 from start to finish on one guitar using the five dynamic levels. Mark the level of each section on your chart.
4. Find the quietest moment in three songs you love. Note what comes immediately after it.

## Common mistakes

- **Loud from start to finish.** Constant intensity sounds smaller than contrast, because the ear adjusts.
- **Adding instead of removing.** When a section needs to feel bigger, try making the section before it smaller.
- **Saving nothing for the end.** Decide what the last chorus gets that no other section has, before you arrange the rest.
