## The idea

To write riffs you need two tools: a way to **write rhythm down** so you can keep and change your ideas, and a basic understanding of **what the drummer is doing**, because a riff is always heard against the drums.

**Note lengths.** Each value is half of the one before:

| Note | Lasts | How many fit in a 4/4 bar |
|---|---|---|
| Whole note | 4 beats | 1 |
| Half note | 2 beats | 2 |
| Quarter note | 1 beat | 4 |
| 8th note | half a beat | 8 |
| 16th note | a quarter of a beat | 16 |

A **dot** makes a note half as long again: a dotted quarter lasts one and a half beats. A **tie** joins two notes into one longer sound. A **rest** is silence with a length. Rests are part of the rhythm.

**The counting grid.** Count 16th notes as "1 e & a, 2 e & a, 3 e & a, 4 e & a". Every possible position in the bar has a name:

```
1  e  &  a  2  e  &  a  3  e  &  a  4  e  &  a
```

Any rhythm in 4/4 with nothing faster than a 16th can be written as hits and gaps on this grid. That is exactly what the grid below is: each cell is one 16th note.

**The drum kit has three jobs:**

| Drum | Job | Typical place |
|---|---|---|
| Kick (bass drum) | The low punch. Marks the strong points. | Beats 1 and 3, plus extra hits |
| Snare | The backbeat. The crack that defines the groove. | Beats 2 and 4 |
| Hi-hat or ride | The clock. Shows the subdivision. | Steady 8ths or 16ths |

**Five patterns cover most rock.** Load each one and listen. Then change it.

## On your instrument

- **Lock with the kick.** The tightest-sounding riffs put their accents exactly where the kick drum plays. When you write a riff, decide the kick pattern at the same time.
- **Leave the snare alone, or hit it on purpose.** A chord that lands with the snare on 2 or 4 sounds huge. A busy riff that covers every snare hit sounds flat, because nothing stands out.
- **Write your riffs on the grid.** Use the grid in the next lesson, or 16 boxes on paper. Tab shows where to put your fingers. The grid shows when.

## Worked example

Writing down a syncopated riff rhythm. Say the rhythm out loud first: "DUM … da DUM … da DUM, DUM … da DUM … da DUM".

Now find each hit on the counting grid:

```
1  e  &  a  2  e  &  a  3  e  &  a  4  e  &  a
X  .  .  X  .  .  X  .  X  .  .  X  .  .  X  .
```

The hits are on 1, the "a" of 1, the "&" of 2, then the same three places in the second half of the bar. The groups are 3 + 3 + 2 sixteenths, twice. Here it is with drums. The kick doubles some of the riff accents and the snare stays on 2 and 4:

## Exercises

1. Clap each of the five drum-pattern grids while it plays: first the kick line, then the snare line, then both, with your foot on the beat.
2. Take three riffs you know and write their rhythm on the 16-step counting grid, one bar each.
3. Program your own one-bar beat in the last grid above. Then write a riff rhythm on the riff line that hits with the kick at least three times.
4. Play any riff you know over the half-time pattern instead of the basic rock beat. Notice that you changed nothing and it became heavier.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing riffs without imagining the drums.** The same riff over a rock beat and a half-time beat is two different parts.
- **Filling every 16th.** A wall of notes has no rhythm. The gaps make the pattern.
- **Ignoring rests when counting.** Count through the silence. The rest is as long as the note would have been.
