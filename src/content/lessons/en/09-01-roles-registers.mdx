A song is chords, melody and lyrics. An **arrangement** is who plays what, when, and where in the frequency spectrum. This is how demos become records.

## The idea

Think of a band as **jobs**, not instruments.

| Role | Who usually does it | What it provides |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Bass and kick drum | The low end, the roots, the rhythmic lock |
| Pulse | Drums, rhythm guitar or keyboard comping | The subdivision and the groove |
| Harmony bed | Rhythm guitar chords, piano, pads | The chords, sustained or rhythmic |
| Melody | Voice or lead instrument | The one thing listeners follow |
| Counter-melody and hooks | A second guitar, keys, backing vocals | Answers in the gaps of the melody |
| Ear candy | Anything | One-off details, transitions, textures |

Any instrument can take any job, and jobs can move between sections. What matters is that every job is covered and that no two parts fight for the same one.

**The frequency ladder.** Every part needs its own zone.

| Zone | Roughly | Who lives there |
|---|---|---|
| Low end | 40–200 Hz | Kick and bass. Nothing else should be busy here. |
| Low mids | 200–500 Hz | The body of guitars, piano left hand, low vocals. Mud collects here. |
| Mids | 500 Hz – 2 kHz | Guitars, snare, vocals, piano. The most crowded zone. |
| Presence | 2–5 kHz | Vocal clarity, pick attack, the "bite" of a lead guitar |
| Air | above 5 kHz | Cymbals, breath, shimmer |

If piano and guitar both play full chords in the same octave, you get mud. The fixes are simple:

- Give the piano high voicings while the guitar plays low power chords, or the reverse.
- Use the small triads from Module 3 instead of six-string barre chords.
- Remove doubled notes. If the bass plays the root, the guitar does not need it.

**One melody at a time.** Two lead lines at once fight for attention. Counter-melodies go in the **rests** of the melody. This is call and response at the level of the whole band.

## On your instrument

Guitar and piano together cover the whole range, which makes them the ideal pair for practicing arrangement.

- **Split 1:** guitar plays a low riff or power chords. Piano plays high, sparse triads and a counter-hook.
- **Split 2:** piano left hand plays the bass line and mid chords. Guitar, with a capo, plays high arpeggios.

With two guitars and no piano, do the same thing: one guitar low and rhythmic, one high and sparse. With only one guitar, record two tracks.

## Worked example

An arrangement chart for a four-section song. Each row is a job and each column is a section.

| Role | Verse 1 | Pre-chorus | Chorus | Bridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Bass on roots, simple kick | Bass adds 8th notes | Bass and kick locked, driving | Bass drops out |
| Pulse | Closed hi-hat | Toms build | Open hi-hat, crashes | Ride cymbal only |
| Harmony bed | Guitar 1, palm-muted power chords | Guitar 1 opens up | Both guitars, full chords | Clean guitar arpeggios |
| Melody | Vocal, low | Vocal, rising | Vocal, high, doubled | Vocal, quiet |
| Counter | None | Guitar 2, one rising line | Guitar 2, octave hook between vocal lines | Piano, high single notes |

Read across any row and the part changes at every boundary. Read down any column and every job is either covered or deliberately empty. No two sections have the same line-up. That is the whole method.

## Exercises

1. Take Assignment Song 1. Write a one-page arrangement chart like the one above: for each section, list which roles are active and who covers them. Make sure no two sections have the same line-up.
2. Record a two-layer demo (a phone is fine): a rhythm guitar part and a second part in a **different register**. Then record a deliberately bad version where both share a register. Hear the mud. You will never unhear it.
3. Listen to a favorite record through one job at a time: only the bass for a whole song, then only the hi-hat and cymbals, then only what answers the vocal.

## Common mistakes

- **Everyone plays all the time.** If every instrument is in from bar 1, the song cannot grow.
- **Two guitars playing the same thing in the same place.** Double a part on purpose, for thickness. Do not do it because nobody wrote a second part.
- **Fixing arrangement problems in the mix.** If two parts mask each other, change the parts.
