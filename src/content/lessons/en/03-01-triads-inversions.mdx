The goal of this module: stop memorizing chord shapes and start **constructing** chords from intervals. Then you can voice any chord anywhere on the guitar or the piano.

## The idea

A triad is a root, a 3rd and a 5th: two 3rds stacked on top of each other. There are four types.

| Triad | Formula | Stacked 3rds | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major | 1 3 5 | major 3rd + minor 3rd | Bright, resolved |
| Minor | 1 ♭3 5 | minor 3rd + major 3rd | Dark, resolved |
| Diminished | 1 ♭3 ♭5 | minor 3rd + minor 3rd | Tense, unstable |
| Augmented | 1 3 ♯5 | major 3rd + major 3rd | Dreamlike, unresolved |

Build any of them on any root. Switch the inversion and listen to how the same three notes change color:

**Inversions** use the same notes with a different one in the bass:

| Position | Bass note | C major | Written |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root position | Root | C E G | C |
| 1st inversion | 3rd | E G C | C/E |
| 2nd inversion | 5th | G C E | C/G |

Inversions matter because they create **bass lines**. C – C/E – F gives the bass a walk up C, E, F instead of a jump. The piano part of "Let It Be" does the mirror image on the way down: F – C/E – Dm – C. Slash chords are the songwriter's tool for smooth, purposeful bass movement under simple harmony.

## On your instrument

**Guitar.** Learn triads on three string sets: 1-2-3, 2-3-4 and 3-4-5. For each set, learn major and minor in all three inversions as small movable shapes. These little triads are the rhythm-guitar vocabulary of funk, pop and indie, and the twin-lead vocabulary of metal. Big open chords are for strumming. Small triads are for **arranging**.

C major on strings 3-2-1, in three places on the neck:

```
     root pos.   1st inv.   2nd inv.
e|-----3-----------8-----------12----|
B|-----5-----------8-----------13----|
G|-----5-----------9-----------12----|
       C E G       E G C       G C E
```

To make any of them minor, lower the 3rd (the E) by one fret.

**Piano.** Play C, C/E and C/G up and down with the right hand. Then take Am – F – C – G and connect the chords with the smallest possible hand movement: Am (A C E), F (A C F, one finger moves), C (G C E), G (G B D).

## Worked example

I – V – vi – IV in C on the top three strings, staying inside a three-fret area:

```
       C       G       Am      F
e|-----3-------3-------5-------5-----|
B|-----5-------3-------5-------6-----|
G|-----5-------4-------5-------5-----|
```

No chord is more than two frets from the one before. Am and F share two notes, so only one finger moves. Compare that with jumping between open C, G, Am and F cowboy chords: same harmony, completely different level of polish.

Now a bass line made from inversions. Play G – D/F♯ – Em – C and listen to the lowest notes: G, F♯, E, C. The descending bass is the hook, and the chords on top are ordinary.

## Exercises

1. On paper, spell major, minor, diminished and augmented triads on all 12 roots. Fifteen minutes, once.
2. Guitar: play C major in all three inversions on strings 1-2-3. From each one, find the nearest F major shape, then the nearest G. You are now voice-leading on guitar.
3. Rewrite C – G – Am – F with slash chords so that the bass walks down by step. One answer: C – G/B – Am – Am/G – F, with the bass going C, B, A, G, F.
4. Take any four-chord song you know and play it with three-string triads instead of full chords. Record both versions.

## Common mistakes

- **Always playing all six strings.** A triad has three notes. Doubling them five times makes the part louder, not better.
- **Ignoring the lowest note.** Whatever note is lowest is what the listener hears as the bass. If you play C/G by accident, the chord feels less stable.
