## The idea

**Two guitars: do not double, split.**

- **By register:** guitar 1 plays low power chords, guitar 2 plays higher triads or arpeggios.
- **By rhythm:** one sustains, one chugs.
- **By role:** one plays rhythm, one plays the harmonized line or the hook.

Doubling the same part, tightly, on both sides is a valid choice for a big chorus. Make it a choice.

**Bass: more than roots.**

- The root on beat 1 is the job. Everything else is optional and should serve the groove.
- Add the 5th or the octave for movement, and approach notes: a step or a half step into the next root.
- Lock with the kick drum. A bass line that plays when the kick plays is what makes a groove sound produced.
- Give the bass a small counter-hook in the gaps of a verse.

**Drums: write intentions, not fills.**

| Section | Typical drum approach |
|---|---|
| Verse | Closed hi-hat, tight and controlled |
| Pre-chorus | A build: toms, 16ths on the snare, opening the hi-hat |
| Chorus | Open hi-hat or ride, crashes on the chord changes |
| Breakdown | Half-time: the snare moves to beat 3. Instantly heavier. |

**Keys in a rock band:** pads for glue, high sparkle lines, or unison with the bass for menace. Not full strummed-style chords in the middle register. That is the guitar's job.

**Voice leading between parts.** When the harmony moves, at least one instrument should carry a stepwise line: the top of the guitar triads, or the right hand of the piano. This is what "expensive" arrangements have.

**Harmonized leads.** For a second guitar, play the same melody a 3rd higher **inside the scale**. Some of the 3rds will be major and some minor. That is correct. Do not move every note by the same number of frets.

## On your instrument

- **Play the bass part on guitar,** on the two lowest strings. If the bass line you imagine is boring to play alone, improve it.
- **Tap the kick pattern with your foot** while you play the bass line. Where they disagree, change one of them.
- **Harmonize on one guitar first.** Play the melody and its harmony as two-note chords on adjacent strings. You will hear at once which 3rds need adjusting.

## Worked example

A melody in E minor and its harmony a 3rd above, staying in the scale E F♯ G A B C D:

| Melody | E | F♯ | G | A | B | A | G |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harmony, a 3rd higher | G | A | B | C | D | C | B |
| Type of 3rd | minor | minor | major | minor | minor | minor | major |

The harmony moves in the same shape as the melody, and the type of 3rd changes by itself because the scale decides it. This is exactly how twin-guitar leads work in Iron Maiden and Thin Lizzy.

If a spot sounds wrong, it is usually because the harmony note clashes with the chord underneath. Try a 4th or a 6th at that spot instead.

## Exercises

1. Score 8 bars of Assignment Song 1 for drums (described in words), bass (tab), two guitars and piano. Every part must pass three questions: does it have a job, does it have its own register, does it leave space?
2. Harmonize the main melody of your song in parallel 3rds for a second instrument. Adjust by ear any spots that clash, usually to a 4th or a 6th.
3. Write a bass line for your chorus that hits the root on beat 1 of each bar and approaches every new root by a step or a half step.
4. Describe the drum approach of each section of your song in one sentence, using the table above as a model.

## Common mistakes

- **A bass line that copies the guitar riff all the time.** Unison is powerful, so save it for the moments that need power.
- **Keys and guitars in the same octave.** One of them has to move.
- **Parallel harmony by fret count.** "Three frets higher" everywhere gives wrong notes. Harmonize inside the scale.
